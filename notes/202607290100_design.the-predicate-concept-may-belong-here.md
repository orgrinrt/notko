# The predicate concept may belong in notko

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Status:** pending obligation. Not a decision, not a design, and not started.
**Raised from:** arvo, while auditing why its predicate traits exist.

This is a note for whoever next has reason to work in notko. It records a question that arose
elsewhere, concluded that notko is probably the right home, and stopped short of designing anything
because the placement carries a real constraint that has to be resolved first.

## What was found in arvo

arvo ships two predicate families that do not reference each other.

`arvo/src/predicate.rs` declares `Pred<A>`, `Pred2<A, B>` and `Pred3<A, B, C>`, trait aliases over
`Fn(..) -> Bool`. The predicate arrives as a closure and is called per item. Two callers, both in
`arvo-comb`.

`arvo-numeric-contracts/src/lib.rs:97` declares `pub const trait Predicate { fn test(self) -> Bool; }`
with five marker subtraits and five wrapper types. Here the predicate is the type and the value is the
receiver.

The first can be called but carries no properties, because a closure is not a type a marker can be
attached to. The second carries properties and nothing reads them: grepping the five markers across
the workspace finds them only in their own declarations, their own impls, and one test. Neither family
is complete alone, and each one's completion is the other.

The full statement, with the shipped-code consequence and the evidence, is
`arvo/mock/design_rounds/202607290100_topic.one-predicate-concept-implemented-twice.md`. The
mechanism it rests on is verified from emitted assembly in
`arvo/mock/research/sketches/202607290030_typestate-algorithm-selection/` and
`arvo/mock/research/sketches/202607282230_hlist-arity-dissolution/`.

## Why notko is the likely home

The concept has no numeric content. `Pred2` is generic over arbitrary `A` and `T`, and the shape that
came out best in the sketches carries an argument list as a type-level hlist, which is `notko-hlist`
by decisions D5 and D9 of arvo's ndim round. `IsZero` and `IsPositive` are numeric, but they are one
instantiation of the concept, not the concept.

"A named type that carries what it guarantees, and is callable" sits at the same level as `Maybe`,
`Outcome` and `Boundable`: a foundation vocabulary item that several unrelated consumers want, and
that is currently being reinvented one layer up.

## The constraint that has to be resolved first

`Predicate::test(self) -> Bool`, and `Bool` is declared in `arvo-storage`, which depends on notko. A
trait returning `Bool` cannot live below the crate that declares `Bool`. arvo's own
`arvo-bridge-home-rule.md` states the general form: a bridge trait lives in the lowest layer where its
**return type** is reachable.

notko's idiom at these positions today is bare `bool`, which is its floor as the zero-dependency
foundation: `Maybe::is`, `Maybe::isnt`, `Outcome::is_ok`, and `Maybe::filter<P: FnOnce(&T) -> bool>`
all use it.

So the fork, unresolved:

1. The notko-level predicate returns `bool`, matching notko's existing idiom, and arvo layers its
   `Bool`-returning numeric family above it.
2. The predicate is generic over its output, and each layer instantiates it with the truth type it
   owns.
3. The concept stays in arvo, and notko takes only the hlist argument-list machinery it already owns
   under `notko-hlist`.

None of these has been argued. The point of this note is that the question exists and has a bearing on
what `notko-hlist` grows into.

## What is expected of whoever picks this up

Read arvo's topic file first, because the finding is there and this is only the placement half of it.
Then treat the fork above as the first question, not the last: the return type decides whether there
is anything to move at all.

Nothing here is settled and nothing here is the agent's call. arvo's round is where the predicate
decision gets made; if it lands on notko, this note is the brief.
